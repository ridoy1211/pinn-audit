# SIAM JOSS and collaboration assessment

Checked 5 November 2026 against current official policies and the completed
joint heat-inversion audit. Venue-fit conclusions below are supervisory judgments,
not editorial decisions or acceptance predictions.

## Recommendation

Neither SISC nor SIMODS is a strong immediate target for the current manuscript.
JOSS is a different publication product and the current repository is too young
for its published screening requirements. Contact a suitable professor for
critical scientific review and possible substantive collaboration. Do not offer
authorship solely to obtain a university name or an assumed faster review.

## JOSS evaluation

JOSS is free to read and publish according to its
[official information](https://joss.theoj.org/about). It evaluates research
software; our current results manuscript would not simply be transferred unchanged.

The [submission requirements](https://joss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/submitting.html)
require more than six months of active public development, demonstrated research
use and good open-source practices. Its paper must not focus on new research
results obtained with the software. Our tests, packaging, public archive and CI
are useful strengths, but insufficient for immediate eligibility or impact.

The [GitHub repository metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/ridoy1211/pinn-audit)
records creation on 15 October 2026. At this check it is about three weeks old,
so cannot have the required six-month history. Waiting alone would not establish
reuse, impact or software significance. No change to the experiment is justified
merely to pursue this venue.

JOSS's [AI policy](https://joss.theoj.org/about#ai-usage-policy) also requires
disclosure and substantive human review, validation and design responsibility.
It restricts AI-assisted conversational interactions with editors/reviewers
except translation. This is an additional policy to respect if JOSS is pursued
later, not an obstacle that should be hidden or assumed satisfied.

## Does being unaffiliated necessarily mean slower review

The sources checked do not establish a general causal rule that an unaffiliated
submission takes longer, or that adding a professor shortens it. Policy allowing
unaffiliated authors does not prove absence of bias. Evidence about prestige
effects on recommendations also does not supply a review-duration prediction.
Our provisional journal explicitly accommodates unaffiliated authors in its
[title-page guidance](https://link.springer.com/journal/42979/submission-guidelines).

### Relevant bias study five-question note

Tomkins, Zhang and Heavlin (2017), Reviewer bias in single- versus double-blind
peer review, PNAS, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1707323114.
[Author abstract via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29138317/).
This check used the abstract, not a new full-text methodological appraisal.

1. **Problem:** Whether visibility of author identities affects conference reviews.
2. **Difference:** Controlled comparison of visible and masked author information.
3. **Testing:** WSDM 2017 submissions received reviews from both reviewer conditions.
4. **Evidence:** The abstract reports more favorable recommendations for prestigious
   authors/institutions in the identity-visible condition.
5. **Limits:** This conference study does not establish journal turnaround times
   for independent researchers or the effect of adding a professor to this paper.

## How to involve a professor appropriately

Ask someone with relevant numerical-PDE, inverse-problem or scientific-ML expertise
to assess the equations, data design, reference fairness, uncertainty and novelty.
Share the actual draft and repository, including the scope of AI assistance.

Authorship should reflect substantial intellectual contributions, critical
drafting/revision, final approval and accountability. A professor can make such
contributions after an initial draft exists; a title, routine proofreading or
permission to list a name is not sufficient by itself. See the provisional
journal's [authorship criteria](https://link.springer.com/journal/42979/submission-guidelines#authorship-principles).

Do not promise authorship in exchange for affiliation or presumed speed. If a
substantive collaboration develops, discuss roles and author order explicitly.
The professor's affiliation does not automatically become Ridoy's affiliation;
each author's entry must reflect their own research connection. A genuine new
institutional research arrangement would need to be described accurately.
